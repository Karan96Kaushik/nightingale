# Spanish Core Concepts: A Beginner–Intermediate Reference

This guide covers the essential grammar and structural concepts every Spanish learner needs, organized in a suggested teaching order. Each section includes explanations, patterns, and examples you can pair with your app's speaking, listening, and vocabulary sections.

---

## Part 1: Foundations

### 1.1 Alphabet & Pronunciation

Spanish uses the same 26 letters as English, plus **ñ**. Key pronunciation notes:

| Letter(s) | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a, e, i, o, u | Always the same pure vowel sound (unlike English) | casa, mesa |
| ñ | "ny" as in "canyon" | niño, español |
| ll | "y" sound (varies by region) | llamar, calle |
| rr / initial r | Rolled/trilled r | perro, ratón |
| j | Harsh "h" sound | jugar, ojo |
| h | Always silent | hola, hacer |
| c (before e/i) | "s" sound (Latin America) or "th" (Spain) | cielo, cena |
| g (before e/i) | Harsh "h" sound, like j | gente, gigante |
| qu | "k" sound | queso, qué |

**Accent marks (tildes)** change stress or meaning:
- Words ending in a vowel, **n**, or **s** stress the second-to-last syllable by default (*ca-sa*).
- Words ending in any other consonant stress the last syllable by default (*ha-blar*).
- A written accent overrides the default rule: **está**, **rápido**, **fácil**.
- Accents also distinguish otherwise-identical words: **tú** (you) vs. **tu** (your); **él** (he) vs. **el** (the); **sí** (yes) vs. **si** (if).

### 1.2 Gender & Number

Every noun is either **masculine** or **feminine** — this affects the articles, adjectives, and pronouns used with it.

**General patterns (not absolute rules):**
- Nouns ending in **-o** → usually masculine (*el libro*)
- Nouns ending in **-a** → usually feminine (*la mesa*)
- Nouns ending in **-ción, -sión, -dad, -tud** → almost always feminine (*la nación, la ciudad*)
- Nouns ending in **-ma** (Greek origin) → often masculine (*el problema, el programa*)
- Common exceptions to memorize: *la mano* (hand), *el día* (day), *el mapa* (map)

**Making nouns plural:**
- Ends in a vowel → add **-s** (*casa → casas*)
- Ends in a consonant → add **-es** (*ciudad → ciudades*)
- Ends in **-z** → change to **-ces** (*lápiz → lápices*)

### 1.3 Articles

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Definite singular ("the") | el | la |
| Definite plural ("the") | los | las |
| Indefinite singular ("a/an") | un | una |
| Indefinite plural ("some") | unos | unas |

- *el libro* → *los libros*
- *una mesa* → *unas mesas*
- Special case: feminine nouns starting with a **stressed a-** sound use **el** for euphony, but stay feminine: *el agua* (the water) is still feminine — *el agua fría*.

## Part 2: Pronouns & Verbs

### 2.1 Subject Pronouns

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | yo (I) | nosotros/nosotras (we) |
| 2nd (informal) | tú (you) | vosotros/vosotras (you all — Spain) |
| 2nd (formal) | usted (you) | ustedes (you all) |
| 3rd | él / ella (he/she) | ellos/ellas (they) |

Note: **vosotros** is used mainly in Spain; Latin America uses **ustedes** for both formal and informal "you all." Subject pronouns are often **dropped** in Spanish because verb endings already show who's acting (*hablo* clearly means "I speak," so *yo* is optional).

### 2.2 Ser vs. Estar (both mean "to be")

This is one of the most important distinctions in Spanish.

**Use SER for:**
- Identity/characteristics: *Soy profesor.* (I am a teacher.)
- Physical traits: *Ella es alta.* (She is tall.)
- Origin/nationality: *Somos de México.* (We are from Mexico.)
- Time and dates: *Son las tres.* (It's three o'clock.)
- Permanent traits

**Use ESTAR for:**
- Location: *Estoy en casa.* (I am at home.)
- Temporary states/conditions: *Estoy cansado.* (I am tired.)
- Emotions: *Está feliz.* (She is happy.)
- Ongoing actions (with gerund): *Estoy comiendo.* (I am eating.)

**Memory trick:** SER = characteristics that define what something *is*; ESTAR = states describing how something currently *is doing/located*. The same adjective can change meaning: *Es aburrido* (He's boring — personality) vs. *Está aburrido* (He's bored — current state).

### 2.3 Present Tense Conjugation (Regular Verbs)

Spanish verbs fall into three groups by their infinitive ending: **-ar, -er, -ir**.

**hablar (to speak) — -ar**
| yo | hablo | nosotros | hablamos |
|---|---|---|---|
| tú | hablas | vosotros | habláis |
| él/ella/usted | habla | ellos/ustedes | hablan |

**comer (to eat) — -er**
| yo | como | nosotros | comemos |
|---|---|---|---|
| tú | comes | vosotros | coméis |
| él/ella/usted | come | ellos/ustedes | comen |

**vivir (to live) — -ir**
| yo | vivo | nosotros | vivimos |
|---|---|---|---|
| tú | vives | vosotros | vivís |
| él/ella/usted | vive | ellos/ustedes | viven |

### 2.4 Irregular Verbs & Stem-Changers

Some high-frequency verbs don't follow the regular pattern:

**Fully irregular in the "yo" form:**
- *tener* (to have) → tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen
- *ir* (to go) → voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van
- *ser* (to be) → soy, eres, es, somos, sois, son

**Stem-changing verbs** (the vowel in the stem changes when stressed):
- **e → ie**: *querer* → quiero, quieres, quiere, queremos, queréis, quieren
- **o → ue**: *poder* → puedo, puedes, puede, podemos, podéis, pueden
- **e → i**: *pedir* → pido, pides, pide, pedimos, pedís, piden

Note that the **nosotros** and **vosotros** forms usually keep the original vowel (no stress on that syllable).

### 2.5 Reflexive Verbs

Used when the subject performs an action on itself. Recognizable by the **-se** attached to the infinitive.

*levantarse* (to get (oneself) up):
- me levanto, te levantas, se levanta, nos levantamos, os levantáis, se levantan

Common reflexive verbs: *llamarse* (to be called), *despertarse* (to wake up), *vestirse* (to get dressed), *bañarse* (to bathe).

Example: *Me llamo Ana.* (I call myself Ana / My name is Ana.)

### 2.6 Past Tenses: Preterite vs. Imperfect

Spanish has two main simple past tenses, and choosing between them is a key intermediate skill.

**Preterite** — completed, specific actions in the past:
- *Comí una manzana.* (I ate an apple. — one finished event)
- Regular -ar endings: -é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -asteis, -aron
- Regular -er/-ir endings: -í, -iste, -ió, -imos, -isteis, -ieron

**Imperfect** — ongoing, habitual, or background actions in the past:
- *Comía manzanas todos los días.* (I used to eat apples every day.)
- -ar endings: -aba, -abas, -aba, -ábamos, -abais, -aban
- -er/-ir endings: -ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían

**Rule of thumb:** Preterite answers "what happened?" (a single completed event); imperfect answers "what was happening/used to happen?" (background, description, repetition). They're often combined: *Estaba durmiendo cuando sonó el teléfono.* (I was sleeping [imperfect, background] when the phone rang [preterite, interrupting event].)

### 2.7 Future & Conditional

**Future** (will do): add endings directly to the infinitive: **-é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -án**
- *hablaré* (I will speak), *comerás* (you will eat)
- Common alternative for near future: *ir + a + infinitive* → *Voy a hablar.* (I'm going to speak.)

**Conditional** (would do): add endings to the infinitive: **-ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían**
- *hablaría* (I would speak), *comeríamos* (we would eat)

Both tenses share the same irregular stems (e.g., *tener → tendr-*, *poder → podr-*, *hacer → har-*).

### 2.8 Present Subjunctive

The subjunctive is a **mood**, not just a tense — it expresses doubt, desire, emotion, or hypothetical situations rather than objective fact.

**Formation:** Take the "yo" form of the present indicative, drop the -o, and add "opposite" endings (-ar verbs get -er/-ir endings and vice versa).
- *hablar* → hablo → **hable, hables, hable, hablemos, habléis, hablen**
- *comer* → como → **coma, comas, coma, comamos, comáis, coman**

**Common triggers** (usually after "que"):
- Wishes/wants: *Quiero que vengas.* (I want you to come.)
- Doubt/denial: *Dudo que sea verdad.* (I doubt it's true.)
- Emotion: *Me alegro de que estés aquí.* (I'm glad you're here.)
- Impersonal expressions: *Es importante que estudies.* (It's important that you study.)

For beginner–intermediate learners, focus on recognition and the most common trigger phrases before full mastery.

### 2.9 Commands (Imperative)

**Informal (tú) affirmative commands** often use the él/ella present-tense form:
- *¡Habla!* (Speak!), *¡Come!* (Eat!), *¡Escribe!* (Write!)

**Informal (tú) negative commands** use the subjunctive:
- *¡No hables!* (Don't speak!), *¡No comas!* (Don't eat!)

**Formal (usted/ustedes) commands** — both affirmative and negative — use the subjunctive form:
- *¡Hable!* / *¡No hable!* (Speak! / Don't speak! — formal)

Common irregular tú commands: *di* (di - decir), *ve* (ir/ver), *ten* (tener), *pon* (poner), *sal* (salir), *sé* (ser), *ven* (venir).

## Part 3: Sentence Structure

### 3.1 Adjective Agreement & Placement

Adjectives must match the noun in **gender** and **number**.
- *el libro rojo* / *los libros rojos*
- *la casa roja* / *las casas rojas*

Adjectives ending in **-e** or a consonant don't change for gender, only number:
- *el estudiante inteligente* / *la estudiante inteligente*

**Placement:** Most descriptive adjectives go **after** the noun (*una casa grande*), but some common adjectives go **before** and can shift meaning:
- *un amigo viejo* (an elderly friend) vs. *un viejo amigo* (a longtime friend)
- *grande* shortens to *gran* before singular nouns when placed before: *un gran hombre* (a great man) vs. *un hombre grande* (a big man)

### 3.2 Question Formation

Question words all carry accent marks:
- **qué** (what), **cómo** (how), **dónde** (where), **cuándo** (when), **por qué** (why), **quién/quiénes** (who), **cuál/cuáles** (which), **cuánto/a** (how much)

Yes/no questions typically just use rising intonation or inverted word order, marked with inverted question marks: *¿Hablas español?*

### 3.3 Negation

Basic negation places **no** before the verb: *No hablo francés.*

Other negative words: **nunca** (never), **nada** (nothing), **nadie** (nobody), **ninguno/a** (none), **tampoco** (neither/either)

Spanish allows (and often requires) **double negatives**: *No tengo nada.* (I don't have anything — literally "I don't have nothing.")

### 3.4 Comparatives & Superlatives

**Comparisons of inequality:**
- más...que (more...than): *Ella es más alta que yo.*
- menos...que (less...than): *Es menos caro que el otro.*

**Comparisons of equality:**
- tan...como (as...as): *Es tan inteligente como su hermano.*
- tanto/a/os/as...como (as much/many...as): *Tengo tantos libros como tú.*

**Superlatives:**
- el/la/los/las + más/menos + adjective: *el más rápido* (the fastest)
- Irregular comparatives: *bueno → mejor* (better), *malo → peor* (worse), *grande → mayor* (older/bigger), *pequeño → menor* (younger/smaller)

## Part 4: Connective Tissue

### 4.1 Prepositions — Por vs. Para

Both can translate to "for," but they aren't interchangeable.

**POR** — used for:
- Duration: *Estudié por dos horas.* (I studied for two hours.)
- Cause/reason: *Gracias por tu ayuda.* (Thanks for your help.)
- Movement through/along: *Caminamos por el parque.* (We walked through the park.)
- Exchange: *Pagué diez dólares por el libro.*
- Means: *por teléfono, por correo*

**PARA** — used for:
- Purpose/goal: *Estudio para aprender.* (I study in order to learn.)
- Recipient: *Este regalo es para ti.* (This gift is for you.)
- Deadline: *Es para el lunes.* (It's due Monday.)
- Destination: *Salgo para Madrid.* (I'm leaving for Madrid.)

**Quick test:** if you can replace it with "in order to" or "intended for," use *para*; if it's about cause, duration, or "through," use *por*.

### 4.2 Direct & Indirect Object Pronouns

**Direct object pronouns** (receive the action directly): me, te, lo/la, nos, os, los/las
- *Veo el libro.* → *Lo veo.* (I see it.)

**Indirect object pronouns** (to/for whom): me, te, le, nos, os, les
- *Doy el libro a María.* → *Le doy el libro.* (I give her the book.)

**Placement:** before a conjugated verb, or attached to the end of an infinitive/gerund/affirmative command:
- *Voy a comprarlo* or *Lo voy a comprar.* (I'm going to buy it.)

When both appear together, indirect comes before direct, and **le/les** becomes **se** before **lo/la/los/las**:
- *Se lo doy.* (I give it to him/her.)

### 4.3 Possessives

**Short form (before the noun):** mi(s), tu(s), su(s), nuestro/a(s), vuestro/a(s), su(s)
- *mi casa, tus libros, nuestra familia*

**Long form (after the noun, for emphasis, or standalone):** mío/a(s), tuyo/a(s), suyo/a(s), nuestro/a(s), vuestro/a(s), suyo/a(s)
- *un amigo mío* (a friend of mine), *Es tuyo.* (It's yours.)

### 4.4 Demonstratives

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter (unknown object) |
|---|---|---|---|
| this | este | esta | esto |
| that (near you) | ese | esa | eso |
| that (far away) | aquel | aquella | aquello |

- *este libro* (this book), *esa silla* (that chair), *aquellas montañas* (those mountains over there)
- Neuter forms (*esto, eso, aquello*) refer to ideas or unidentified things: *¿Qué es esto?* (What is this?)

## Part 5: Numbers, Time, and Practical Grammar

### 5.1 Numbers

- 0–10: cero, uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez
- 11–20: once, doce, trece, catorce, quince, dieciséis, diecisiete, dieciocho, diecinueve, veinte
- Tens: treinta, cuarenta, cincuenta, sesenta, setenta, ochenta, noventa, cien
- **Uno** shortens to **un** before masculine nouns: *un libro* (not *uno libro*); becomes **una** before feminine: *una mesa*.
- Numbers 16–29 are usually written as one word (*dieciséis, veintidós*); 31+ use "y" (*treinta y uno*).

### 5.2 Telling Time & Dates

**Time:** Use *Son las...* for most hours, *Es la una* for 1:00.
- *Son las tres.* (It's 3:00.) *Son las tres y media.* (It's 3:30.) *Son las cuatro menos cuarto.* (It's a quarter to four.)

**Dates:** day + de + month + de + year, and cardinal numbers (not ordinal) except for the first:
- *el primero de enero* (the first of January), *el dos de marzo* (March 2nd)
- Days and months are **not capitalized** in Spanish: *lunes, martes... enero, febrero...*

### 5.3 Ir + a + Infinitive (Near Future)

One of the most useful beginner structures — equivalent to English "going to":
- *Voy a estudiar.* (I'm going to study.)
- *Vamos a comer.* (We're going to eat.)

Conjugate *ir* (voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van) + **a** + the infinitive verb. This is often taught before the formal future tense because it's simpler and extremely common in spoken Spanish.

## Part 6: Culture & Context

### 6.1 Regional Variations

- **Vosotros** (informal "you all") is used in Spain; Latin America uses **ustedes** for both formal and informal plural "you."
- **Vos** replaces **tú** in parts of Latin America (notably Argentina, Uruguay, and parts of Central America), with its own verb conjugation: *vos hablás* (instead of *tú hablas*).
- Vocabulary varies by region: *computadora* (Latin America) vs. *ordenador* (Spain) for "computer"; *carro*/*coche* for "car."

### 6.2 Formal vs. Informal Address

- **Tú** — informal, used with friends, family, peers, children
- **Usted** — formal, used with strangers, elders, in professional contexts, or to show respect
- The choice affects verb conjugation (*tú hablas* vs. *usted habla*) and possessives/object pronouns, so it's worth teaching early alongside subject pronouns.

## Suggested Teaching Order Summary

1. Alphabet & pronunciation → Gender & articles
2. Subject pronouns → Ser vs. estar → Present tense (regular, then irregular/stem-changing)
3. Reflexive verbs → Ir + a + infinitive → Numbers, time, dates
4. Adjective agreement → Questions → Negation
5. Preterite vs. imperfect → Object pronouns → Possessives/demonstratives
6. Future & conditional → Comparatives → Por vs. para
7. Present subjunctive → Commands → Regional/cultural context

This keeps high-utility, high-frequency structures early (present tense, near future, basic questions) while saving more nuanced material (subjunctive, preterite vs. imperfect nuance, formal commands) for the intermediate stage.
